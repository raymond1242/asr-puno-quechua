\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{The submitted system has no clean measurement of its own.} It is
trained on the established split, whose training portion contains the
development sentences. We submit it for comparability with the published
baseline and because it sees 15\% more distinct sentences than the
text-disjoint alternative; that more text should help on new sentences is a
reasoned bet, not a measured one, and the 1.1-point effect of
Section~\ref{sec:overlap} measures memorisation and must not be used to justify
it. The clean figures belong to the sibling model trained on the text-disjoint
split.

\textbf{The search was narrow:} we varied updates and learning rate only.
Batch size, spontaneous upsampling, layer dropout, accent folding and the
inclusion of silver data were left at their defaults.

\textbf{Three seeds} are enough to establish that several apparent wins were
noise, too few to separate recipes differing by less than their spread.

\textbf{Half the ranking cannot be measured faithfully.} The spontaneous test
consists entirely of speakers aged sixty or over. In the whole corpus, such
speakers contribute one gold transcription and 2.90 of 35.41 hours, all silver.
No development or held-out set we can construct contains gold elderly
spontaneous speech, so the domain deciding half our rank is the one we can
least evaluate. Gold transcriptions from elderly speakers are, in our view, the
single most valuable addition this resource could receive.
