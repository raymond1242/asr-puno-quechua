\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{The submitted system contains data leakage.} It is trained on the
established split, whose training portion contains the development sentences.
We submit it for comparability with the published baseline and because it sees
15\% more distinct sentences than the text-disjoint alternative. However, there
is data leakage across splits.

\textbf{The search was narrow:} we varied updates and learning rate only.
Batch size, spontaneous upsampling, layer dropout, accent folding and the
inclusion of silver data were left at their defaults.

\textbf{Three seeds} are enough to establish that several apparent wins were
noise, too few to separate configurations differing by less than their spread.

\textbf{Half the ranking cannot be measured faithfully.} The spontaneous test
consists entirely of speakers aged sixty or over. In the whole corpus, such
speakers are a small and almost unlabelled part of it: they account for 2.90 of
its 35.41 hours of spontaneous audio, some 8\%, and across all of those clips
exactly one carries a human transcription. The rest hold only silver labels.
No development or held-out set we can construct therefore contains gold elderly
spontaneous speech, so the domain deciding half our rank is the one we can
least evaluate. Gold transcriptions from elderly speakers are, in our view, the
single most valuable addition this resource could receive.
