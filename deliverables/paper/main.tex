\begin{abstract}
We describe our system for Task~1 of the SIMBig~2026 shared task on automatic
speech recognition for Puno Quechua (ISO 639-3: \texttt{qxp}). Starting from the
published XLS-R-300M checkpoint with continued pre-training, we fine-tune for
500 updates on the organisers' curated reference transcriptions and decode
spontaneous speech with a character $n$-gram language model under lexicon-free
beam search. On the established speaker-disjoint split the system improves the
published baseline from 13.15 to 9.14 mean word error rate.

Two properties of the task proved more consequential than any modelling choice.
The curated transcription conventions released for the task are worth nearly
eight points of scripted word error rate on their own: one fixed set of hypotheses scores 8.13
against the raw Common Voice transcriptions and 16.08 against the curated ones. And the established split shares every
evaluation sentence with training, because the corpus holds 2{,}067 sentences
read by 77 speakers; we measure that hearing a sentence from other speakers is
worth 1.1 points, and show that the shared task's own test does not reuse those
sentences. We therefore also report a speaker- and text-disjoint split, and a
held-out set measured once after freezing every decision. Three negative
results follow: averaging CTC logits across seeds degrades recognition through
a mechanism we identify, stronger masking is monotonically harmful, and the
language model, tuned on development data, no longer helps on held-out data.

\keywords{Automatic speech recognition \and Low-resource languages \and
Quechua \and Evaluation methodology \and Self-supervised learning.}
\end{abstract}
