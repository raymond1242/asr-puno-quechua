\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Read-speech corpora for low-resource languages are built the way this one was:
a small pool of sentences recorded many times over by the few speakers who
volunteer -- here 2{,}067 sentences and 77 speakers. Any split that separates
speakers while using all the recordings therefore evaluates with complete text
overlap, by construction rather than by oversight, since the training portion
must contain every sentence in order to contain every clip. We report this as a
property of the benchmark, and it becomes consequential here because the shared
task's own test draws on sentences outside the pool, so systems are selected
under a different condition from the one they are judged under.

The remedy is cheap: report the text-disjoint figure alongside the conventional
one. Where a corpus ships an official split disjoint in both respects, as
Common Voice does here at the cost of one recording per sentence, the lost
training data buys one honest measurement among the optimistic ones.
