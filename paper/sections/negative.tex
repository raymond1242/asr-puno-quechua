\section{What Did Not Work}
\label{sec:negative}

\subsection{Averaging CTC models across seeds}


Seed noise of 0.3 mean WER is exactly what ensembling should remove, so we
expected a gain. Neither fusion rule delivers one, and the failure has a
measurable mechanism. CTC emits sharp peaks at particular frames and blanks
elsewhere, and independently trained models place those peaks at adjacent but
not identical frames. Averaging logits is a geometric mean over probabilities,
so a confident peak averaged against a near-zero value collapses and the letter
disappears: character deletions rise from 21 to 50, and mean WER rises from
7.02 for a single seed to 8.38. Averaging probabilities stops deleting letters
but then gains nothing, at 7.30, within noise of a single model.

With CTC's peaky, time-shifted alignments, the fusion rule matters more than
the ensemble.

\subsection{Stronger SpecAugment masking}

Overfitting past 1{,}000 updates is the failure mode we observed, and
SpecAugment \cite{park2019specaugment} addresses it directly. Raising the
time-masking probability from the default 0.05 to 0.2 and 0.5 gives 6.81, 7.05
and 7.68 mean WER: monotonically worse, on all four numbers. With a 500-update
schedule there is little time to overfit, and masking removes evidence the
model has few chances to see.

\subsection{The language model gain does not survive}
\label{sec:lmneg}

On scripted speech, no setting in the grid gains measurably once the acoustic
model is this accurate: every configuration sits within $\pm0.15$ WER of greedy
decoding, about two words. We therefore decode scripted speech without a
language model, which is why the final system uses one in only one domain.

On spontaneous speech the model does gain on development data, by 0.31, 0.17
and 0.12 WER across three seeds, but on held-out data the identical
configuration gains 0.00 and loses 0.04 and 0.08. The gain does not transfer,
even though it was tuned on the text-disjoint split. We keep the configuration,
since the held-out effect is within noise either way, but no longer claim it
helps.

An earlier version of this tuning shows the hazard more sharply. Run on the
speaker-disjoint development set, the grid selects $\alpha = 2.0$ and reports
4.54 scripted WER -- at that weight the language model recalls whole
development sentences it was built from. Under the new-sentence condition the
same setting yields 9.83, worse than greedy. A language model tuned where the
text is shared will prefer memorisation, expensively.
